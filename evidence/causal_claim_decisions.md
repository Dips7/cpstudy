# Causal Claim Decisions

Phase 4 audit for manuscript claims. This is a rewrite decision ledger, not a replacement manuscript. The manuscript source remains unchanged until Phase 6. “Causal within the simulation” refers only to controlled policy contrasts under the named configuration and simulator rules; it does not imply field causality.

| Claim or phrase | Evidence and causal status | Decision for rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| Calibrated versus joint policy changes timely success at the 20-seed setting | All 80 runs pass trace audit; shared seeds and common exogenous opportunity demand. Calibrated-minus-joint same-opportunity success is +40.07 percentage points, paired 95% t interval half-width 4.18 points. This supports a policy-assignment contrast within this model at N=48 and 3 ms. | Retain as a model-conditional comparative result. State n, setting, policy definitions, and that both are simulated controllers. Avoid “mitigates false alarms” as a general mechanism. |
| Fixed and radio policies outperform feedback policies in that paired setting | Paired per-seed records support the measured contrast: calibrated is 38.83 pp below fixed and 26.07 pp below radio. `radio` is the project's internal CBR controller. | Retain as a bounded result for this operating point. Do not conclude sender-side control is universally wrong or that radio policy is ETSI DCC. |
| 3 ms and 6 ms stress outcomes show a general bottleneck transition | Recomputed offered load is 0.571 mean at 3 ms and 1.143 at 6 ms for fixed-policy arrival traces. But ten stress traces are incomplete; some cells have n=1 or n=2. Offered rho is not measured busy time. | Describe the recomputed offered-load pattern and audited stress outcomes with cell-specific n. Do not claim a universal physical transition or attribute every policy's result directly to offered rho. |
| “93.38% of rate cuts were caused by isolated neighbor backlog bursts” | Trigger attribution classifies 1,242/1,330 joint decreases as `backlog_only` (93.38%). It does not establish isolation, outlier status, or that these trigger decisions caused the total success-rate difference. | Retain only “93.38% of observed joint decreases had a backlog-only trigger classification.” Remove “isolated,” “outlier,” “trap,” and causal mechanism phrasing. |
| “86.2% of safety updates were suppressed” | No verified denominator/source supports this percentage; opportunity updates are not safety updates. | Remove. Do not replace without an auditable numerator, denominator, and operational definition. |
| “100% of cryptographic processor cycles are wasted on expired frames” | The event model runs verification before classifying expiration, but no run-level service-time numerator/denominator was calculated. It represents synthetic delay, not processor cycles or automotive firmware. Queue depth arithmetic does not establish that every frame is expired at service start. | Remove the empirical percentage and real-firmware claim. State the implementation rule narrowly: the modeled worker does not prune expired frames before verification; expiry is checked after service. |
| “Theorem 2” gives a 66.7% TOSR upper bound | The calculation assumes a fixed homogeneous cluster and equal service allocation; its 25-node count includes the receiver despite self-reception being excluded. It also conflates a service-load constraint with timely success under a dynamic radio, deadline, and queue model. | Remove Theorem 2 and the claimed empirical-theoretical gap. If useful, include only a boxed conditional load arithmetic example: for 24 equal senders, 6 ms deterministic service, and one receiver, offered load below one requires each sender below 6.94 Hz. This is not a TOSR bound or simulated prediction. |
| “Sender throttling causes perception starvation” | Lower admission rates mechanically reduce emitted updates in the periodic opportunity fixture; no perception-quality or safety endpoint is modeled. Success contrasts are policy-conditional and do not identify a real perception effect. | Replace with “rate reduction lowered the measured same-opportunity update availability in this simulation under the tested setting.” Do not call it perception starvation or safety impact. |
| “Calibrated controller uses temporal moving-average smoothing” | Code averages currently fresh neighbor report values at a decision instant (cross-sectional mean), not a time-window average. | Correct terminology to “mean backlog across fresh neighbor reports at each control update.” |
| “ETSI DCC achieved X / standardized DCC” | The `dcc` policy implements a bounded adaptive core and single-service mapping; it is not a full ITS-G5 conformance implementation. | Use “project DCC mapping” or “DCC-inspired comparator.” Explicitly exclude it from the 20-seed paired comparison. |
| “The channel was physically clear while computers falsely throttled” | A simulator CBR trace and trigger log support measured values and policy decisions in the selected run. “Physically clear,” “false alarm,” and general cause are interpretive and model-specific. | Report observed CBR/trigger traces only with the exact subset and avoid causal language beyond controller logic. |
| “100 ms is a strict safety deadline” / “results improve road safety” | Deadline is a study design value. There is no perception quality, collision, or safety endpoint. | Call it the configured 100 ms simulation deadline. Remove safety-critical and deployment recommendations. |
| “Spatial dispersion protects receiver queues” | Synthetic positions and observed queue outcomes support a description of the simulated geometry and traces. They do not generalize to real intersections or prove spatial dispersion as a general cause. | Say “in the simulated intersection configuration, the fixed-policy offered-load estimates were …” Avoid “naturally protects.” |
| “Queue-aware threshold 16 is optimal” | Five-seed ablation shows the highest observed mean among four tested thresholds; these runs were exploratory and selection is outcome-informed. | Say it had the highest mean among the tested thresholds in this ablation. Do not use “optimal,” “systematic,” or imply an independent validation set. |
| “Vehicular architectures must adopt Verify-on-Demand (VoD), kinematic filtering, or deadline-aware pruning” | These remedies were not implemented or compared. The model has no perception/hazard truth or safety endpoint from which to prioritize “high-risk” vehicles. | Remove prescriptive safety-architecture recommendations. They may be listed as untested research directions, clearly separate from study findings. |

## Claim ledger synchronization

- C008 is narrowed to trigger classification, not isolated-burst causation.
- C009 remains removed due to unsupported denominator and misuse of “safety updates.”
- C014 is removed as a theorem; any conditional arithmetic example belongs in the model assumptions section with explicit premises.
- C015 is removed as an empirical cycle percentage.
- C016 is restated as a synthetic service-time budget.
- C017 is removed: the Phase 2 implementation audit already disproved the manuscript's unique-association proposition for freshness coverage.
- C021 is limited to simulated timing outcomes; safety/deployment claims are not supported.
- C026-C031 record new controller, model-scope, broad-causality, architecture-recommendation, and historical-provenance decisions identified in this phase.
