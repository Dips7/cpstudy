# Related Work Draft (Phase 5)

Cooperative-perception research has examined how message content and transmission frequency affect channel use and the information available to receiving vehicles. Thandavarayan et al. evaluated congestion control in cooperative-perception scenarios, while a separate study by the same authors examined generation and organization of cooperative-perception messages [2, 3]. These works establish relevant communication and message-generation contexts. The present study addresses a narrower question: how sender policies compare when a receiver's verification queue is represented by an explicit synthetic service delay and delayed receiver-load feedback is available.

Queueing and security overhead have also been studied in broadcast vehicular communication. Muhammad et al. developed an analytical treatment of security-related delay across sender, radio, and receiver stages [1]. Age-based scheduling work has considered collaborative perception with region-level freshness constraints and asynchronous source updates [6]. Chen et al. developed Age of Computing metrics for communication and computation cooperative networks with deadline models [7]. These papers provide context for queueing and freshness measures, but their system models and outcomes differ from the opportunity-based simulation metric used here.

Standards documents define relevant message and access-layer mechanisms, including ETSI's Collective Perception Service and decentralized congestion-control specification [4, 5]. They are standards context rather than validation of this study's simulator: the project DCC policy is a limited mathematical mapping, and the verification delay is a configured synthetic input rather than measured cryptographic processing. This paper therefore reports a comparative simulation characterization of radio contention and receiver-feedback behavior under its stated assumptions. It does not claim a new queueing or freshness metric, a complete standards implementation, hardware calibration, or vehicle-safety validation.
